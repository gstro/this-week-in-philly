“The Dispossessed” Book Club: Session #8 (Final session)
The final session of an eight-part read-through of Ursula K. Le Guin's The Dispossessed — her 1974 anarchist-utopia novel, one of the genre's foundational political texts — closes out with the group landing on the book's ambiguous politics rather than just recapping plot. *(confirm details)*: the listing gives no address beyond "Philadelphia," so confirm the location before heading over.

✨ NFC Sculpture Workshop

Center City/Fitler Square, an easy walk from Rittenhouse.

Little Bighorn at 150: Native Nations and the Fate of the American West

At the National Constitution Center in Old City, an easy walk from Center City.

Tuesday November 24

⭐ Top 3 Picks

1
Justice 101: From Natural Rights to “Slave of the State”- Philadelphia’s Founding Vision and the Making of the “Slave of the Sta
Eastern State Penitentiary's lecture series turns its own history inside out this time, tracing a line from Philadelphia's founding "natural rights" rhetoric to the carceral state the city went on to help build — the kind of institution-critiquing public history that's easy to walk past. A one-off talk inside one of the city's most resonant sites for thinking about incarceration and state power. Fairmount — take the 48 bus or walk about 20 minutes from the Broad Street Line's Fairmount Ave stop.
Eastern State Penitentiary
5:30 PM · Not listed
2
Flipphone Presents: Latrice Royale: WHY IT GOTTA BE WHITE CHRISTMAS?!?!
Latrice Royale — a standout from RuPaul's Drag Race, by reputation — brings a holiday-themed send-up of White Christmas to City Winery for one night. Broad, campy, big-personality queer performance that's worth the ticket regardless of the room it's in. Center City, Chinatown.
City Winery
7:30 PM · Not listed
3
Film | Ninotchka
A rare big-screen outing for Ninotchka, Ernst Lubitsch's 1939 comedy where Greta Garbo plays a stone-faced Soviet envoy who melts in gay Paris — witty, quietly political, and exactly the kind of repertory programming a commercial multiplex would never touch. Chestnut Hill — reachable via Regional Rail's Chestnut Hill East Line or the 23 bus up Germantown Ave.
Woodmere Art Museum
7:00 PM · Not listed

Saturday November 28

⭐ Top 3 Picks

1
Harriett's Deeply Rooted Trolley Tour: Small Biz Saturday Edition
Harriett's Bookshop — the Fishtown shop built around Black and women authors and named for Harriet Tubman — doesn't show up in Collection most weeks, so a rare appearance is worth taking. This is their Small Business Saturday trolley tour, moving shoppers between Black- and women-owned storefronts rather than steering foot traffic to the usual chains. Fishtown, near the Market-Frankford Line's Girard stop or the 15 trolley on Girard Ave.
Harriett's Bookshop
9:30 AM · Not listed
2
Dark Sufers (NJ), Shampoo, Sappers, The Speed Dials & TTLC
A five-band punk and power-pop bill at Vox Populi, the long-running artist-run collective space, by reputation — Dark Sufers come down from New Jersey to headline, backed by local openers Shampoo, Sappers, The Speed Dials, and TTLC. A few blocks north of the Convention Center, near the Broad-Ridge Spur's Chinatown stop or Jefferson Station.
Vox Populi (319 N 11th St, Philadelphia, PA 19107-1305)
6:30 PM · Not listed
3
Licorice Pizza (2021)
A rare theatrical rerun of Paul Thomas Anderson's Licorice Pizza — his 1970s San Fernando Valley coming-of-age story, one of the sharpest and funniest things he's made — at the Philadelphia Film Society's Chestnut Street screen. The kind of repertory booking that disappears fast once a studio's licensing window closes. Center City, Chestnut Street.
PFS Film Society Center, 1412 Chestnut Street, Philadelphia, PA 19102
7:00 PM · Not listed

⭐ Top 3 Picks

1
Andrzej Żuławski Book Release ft. L’AMOUR BRAQUE (1985)
PhilaMOCA screens Andrzej Żuławski's 1985 L'Amour Braque (Mad Love) — one of the wilder entries in the Polish director's hysterical, maximalist filmography — to celebrate critic Samm Deighan's new book on his work, with Deighan herself in the room. Doors/reception at 7, screening introduction at 7:30. Exactly the kind of specialist film-and-literature crossover that justifies PhilaMOCA's whole existence. $12, Callowhill, an easy walk from the Convention Center or Jefferson Station.
PhilaMOCA, 531 N 12th St, Philadelphia, PA 19123
7:00 PM · $12

A Jam Without Borders (Fundraiser for Juntos)
A jazz jam session at Ortlieb's raising money for Juntos, the South Kensington-based immigrant-rights and workers' organization — led by Corey Bernhard, with the usual Sunday-night Ortlieb's crowd of serious players sitting in. Real stakes behind a low-key night of music. Northern Liberties, near the Market-Frankford Line's Girard stop or the 57 bus.
Ortlieb's
