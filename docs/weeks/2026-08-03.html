⭐ Top 3 Picks
1
DEAD MEDIA (w/ filmmaker Joseph Scrimshaw in attendance)
DEAD MEDIA is a horror-comedy about a haunted DVD and a movie night that refuses to end — a gnarly, affectionate homage to cult horror and the bygone era of DVD bonus features. Writer-director Joseph Scrimshaw is doing the screening in person, so you get the rare chance to grill the guy responsible for the haunted disc right after the credits roll. PhilaMOCA's cramped, velvet-dark former mausoleum showroom is exactly the room this movie was born to play in — doors around 7:00 PM, screening at 7:30.
PhilaMOCA
7:30 PM · $12–$13.20
2
The Writing Room
Three silent 40-minute writing sprints with 10-minute breaks and accountability check-ins between them — bring your own coffee and whatever project you've been avoiding, no genre or experience required. Iffy Books is South Philly's anarchist-adjacent DIY bookstore and reading room, so the space itself carries the same restless independent-press energy that makes it worth a visit even on a night with nothing scheduled. Free and low-key; you just have to actually write for two hours instead of talking about writing.
Iffy Books
5:00 PM · Not listed (likely free)
3
Rustin's Challenge Reading Group
A discussion of 'Rustin's Challenge,' which reconstructs Bayard Rustin's little-known economic vision — a jobs-centered 'Freedom Budget for All Americans' — as an alternative to what the organizers call the left's 'maximalist sloganeering.' Led by Paul Prescod at the Ethical Society's Rittenhouse Square home, it's the rare political reading group actually arguing tactics and history rather than trading vibes. Free, RSVP-based, and runs a tight two hours (6–8 PM) — easy to walk into cold even if you haven't read the book.
Philadelphia Ethical Society
6:00 PM · Free (RSVP required)

⭐ Top 3 Picks
1
NLG Know Your Rights Training
The National Lawyers Guild runs a hands-on know-your-rights training at Philly's own anarchist radical bookstore, teaching what to do if police or ICE show up at your door, your job, or a protest. It's exactly the kind of practical, unglamorous organizing work that matters more this year than most, and Wooden Shoe has been a hub for exactly this since 1976 — no membership or affiliation required, just show up and learn. A rare case where a free evening at a bookstore might genuinely be useful to have sat through.
Wooden Shoe Books
6:00 PM · Free (typical for NLG trainings; not listed on page)
2
Touki Bouki
Djibril Diop Mambéty's 1973 Senegalese classic — a fever-dream about a cowherder and a student scheming to escape postcolonial Dakar for Paris — gets an honest-to-god theatrical screening as part of PFS's summer Road Trip series, rather than the usual grainy rip. It's routinely called one of African cinema's most audacious works, part political satire, part afro-punk fashion show. A 4:00 PM matinee is also available if evening doesn't work, but the 7:00 PM slot is the main event.
PFS Film Society Center
7:00 PM · ~$12–14 (standard PFS ticket price; not listed)
3
Analog A/V Session
Bring your dusty VHS tapes and cassette bootlegs — Iffy Books sets you loose with real analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog transfer gear, a scrappy hands-on session that fits nowhere else on the week's calendar. It's the exact overlap Iffy consistently delivers on: DIY electronics culture in a leftist-adjacent indie bookstore, run on a pay-what-you-can basis so nobody's priced out. Good excuse to finally digitize that box of mixtapes.
Iffy Books
10:30 AM · Free/PWYW (suggested donation $5–$15, notaflof)
