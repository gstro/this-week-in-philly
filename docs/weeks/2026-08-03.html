1
Abronia, Cartoon +Bill Nace, Earthen Jug
Portland's Abronia rolls through on the East Coast leg of its tour behind the new LP Shapes Unravel, a shapeshifting blend of desert-noir drone, avant-jazz, doom, and acid-folk that's hard to categorize and harder to skip. The bill is stacked for a random Monday: kraut-punk locals Cartoon, genuinely legendary noise guitarist Bill Nace (Body/Head, decades of free-improv pedigree), and the drone-psych ensemble Earthen Jug. Ortlieb's is a small, no-frills room built for exactly this kind of show, and under $20 covers all four acts.
Ortlieb's
7:00 PM · $19.74
2
Crooklyn (1994)
cinéSPEAK's 'Two Locals Movie Mondays' series brings Spike Lee's Crooklyn — his warm, lived-in portrait of a Brooklyn childhood, anchored by knockout turns from Alfre Woodard and Delroy Lindo — to Philly's first Black-owned brewery. It's a makeup date after a summer reschedule, free to attend with BYO dinner and free popcorn, donations of $5+ welcomed to keep the series running. This is the neighborhood-cinema-meets-community-taproom event that's genuinely easy to miss if you're not watching for it.
Two Locals Brewing
7:00 PM · Free (suggested $5+ donation)

Sonder x Poison Tree Magazine Launch
Two brand-new Philly literary magazines, Sonder and Poison Tree, share a launch party at Tattooed Mom, with early arrivals getting first crack at a collaborative guest book and physical copies of issue one. The back half of the night opens the floor to an open mic — flash fiction, poetry, short essays from whoever wants to read — a genuinely grassroots, no-cover night for anyone plugged into (or curious about) the city's small-press scene.

DEAD MEDIA
A haunted DVD, a lonely Gen X uncle, and a movie night that refuses to end — DEAD MEDIA is a loving, gnarly homage to grindhouse horror and the lost art of the DVD bonus feature, and writer/director Joseph Scrimshaw will be there in person to talk about it afterward. It's the kind of intimate, in-conversation screening you can't get at a multiplex, staged at PhilaMOCA, Philly's cathedral for cult and underground film. Twelve bucks gets you in for a movie you'll be describing to friends for weeks.
PhilaMOCA

Rustin's Challenge Reading Group
Everyone knows Bayard Rustin organized the March on Washington; almost nobody knows he also drafted a "Freedom Budget" for full employment and economic justice that could've reshaped the American left. This discussion group, led by contributor Paul Prescod, digs into a new essay collection reassessing that vision at the Ethical Society's Rittenhouse Square rowhouse — the kind of small, serious political gathering that never makes the big listings but rewards the people who show up. Free, 6-8pm, no ticket required.

⭐ Top 3 Picks
1
NLG Know Your Rights Training
The National Lawyers Guild runs a hands-on know-your-rights training at Wooden Shoe Books, walking attendees through concrete legal footing for encounters with police, ICE, and immigration enforcement. It's the kind of unglamorous, essential organizing work that never trends but matters enormously right now, and hosting it inside Philly's longtime anarchist infoshop on South Street guarantees a room full of people who take it seriously. The listing is thin on specifics beyond time and place, so double-check format and any RSVP requirements before heading over.
Wooden Shoe Books
6:00 PM
2
Touki Bouki
Djibril Diop Mambéty's 1973 Senegalese landmark follows a cowherder and a university student scheming to escape postcolonial Dakar for Paris, told in fever-dream imagery with a political charge that hasn't dulled in fifty years — one of the essential works of African and world cinema. It's screening as part of Philadelphia Film Society's summer Road Trip series, a genuinely rare theatrical booking for a film that mostly lives in retrospectives and syllabi, with two showtimes (4:00 and 7:00 PM) presented in Wolof, Arabic, and French with subtitles. No ticket price is listed on this page, so confirm cost through PFS directly.
Philadelphia Film Society
4:00 PM, 7:00 PM
3
Rolling Quartz
South Korean rock band Rolling Quartz — five members deep into riffs sturdier than most K-pop-adjacent crossover acts attempt — bring their "My Turn" reunion tour to PhilaMOCA, a converted mausoleum-turned-DIY art/horror venue about as far from an arena stage as touring bands get. Catching an international act of this scale in a room built for a few hundred people is a genuine rarity, and PhilaMOCA's oddball, community-run character (usually horror screenings and underground art) makes for a memorably strange pairing. Tiered ticketing runs from general admission up through VIP soundcheck/meet-and-greet packages — steep for the venue, but reflective of a real touring draw rather than a local bar band.
PhilaMOCA
6:00 PM (doors), 7:00 PM (show) · $58–$250
