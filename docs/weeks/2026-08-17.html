Monday August 17

⭐ Top 3 Picks

1
Philadelphia Psychotronic Film Society
PhilaMOCA's Psychotronic Film Society hits its regular first/third-Monday slot with a night of deliberately deranged programming — the kind of gonzo, grindhouse-adjacent bill you won't find at a multiplex. It's $5 at the door, no advance ticket needed, in the tiny mausoleum-turned-microcinema on N. 12th St. The exact film is kept loose by design (the venue just warns of 'extreme subject matter') — part of the appeal is not knowing until you're in the seat.
PhilaMOCA, 531 N 12th St, Philadelphia, PA 19123
7:30 PM · $5 At Door
2
☕️ Coffee Talk
A standing first-and-third-Monday hangout for trans and non-binary people (plus partners and allies) at Iffy Books, the anarchist bookstore that's become one of the city's steadiest queer-community anchors. No RSVP, no cover — just snacks, coffee, and a room built explicitly to be safe. Small and recurring, but exactly the kind of low-visibility community infrastructure that's easy to overlook and worth knowing about.
Iffy Books, 404 S. 20th St., Philadelphia, 19146, United States
6:00 PM · Not listed
3
Fraternal Twin x Ditch x Lo Fives x Wax Girl
A DIY garage-rock/shoegaze bill at Cousin Danny's, the scrappy West Philly lounge that punches above its size for touring indie acts. Fraternal Twin and Ditch are passing through on a broader tour (they'd just played Pittsburgh), backed by locals Lo Fives and Wax Girl — a genuinely easy-to-miss weeknight show off the big-venue circuit.
Cousin Danny's (5001 Market Street)
7:30 PM · Not listed

Tuesday August 18

⭐ Top 3 Picks

1
Tim Kasher’s WHO’S WATCHING
Cursive frontman Tim Kasher's directorial feature gets a rare theatrical stop at PhilaMOCA — an advance screening of his psychological horror-thriller 'Who's Watching,' about a metalhead's obsessive fixation on a coworker, ahead of its wider September release. Kasher sticks around after for a Q&A and a short acoustic set, which is the real reason to go: a one-night convergence of horror-film-nerd and indie-rock-fan Philly, not something you can catch next month. $17 at the tiny mausoleum-turned-microcinema on N. 12th St.
PhilaMOCA, 531 N 12th St, Philadelphia, PA 19123
7:30 PM · $17
2
Black Pontiac and Noshows
Vancouver's Black Pontiac bring their self-described 'DIY or die' melodic garage-rock and indie-pop to Ortlieb's, with Sunderstar and Philly's own Noshows opening. A real touring bill at a real Northern Liberties room, not a hometown filler night. $20.55, 21+, doors at 8.
Ortlieb's, Philadelphia, PA
8:00 PM · $20.55, 21+
3
Rustin's Challenge Reading Group
A free, in-person discussion of 'Rustin's Challenge' — the book reframing Bayard Rustin, the organizer who actually built the March on Washington, around his 'Freedom Budget for All Americans' — led with Paul Prescod at the Ethical Society. Political education in the same lineage as Wooden Shoe's ed line: history read for what it can teach an organizing movement right now, not trivia about a famous speech.
Philadelphia Ethical Society, Philadelphia, PA
6:00 PM · Not listed

Wednesday August 19

⭐ Top 3 Picks

1
BOOK TALK: "The People's Historian: The Outsized Life of Howard Zinn"
Dave Zirin's new biography of Howard Zinn gets a stop at Wooden Shoe, the city's anarchist bookstore and the natural home for it — 'The People's Historian' traces how the author of 'A People's History of the United States' became one of the 20th century's defining radical intellectuals. Exactly the kind of political-education programming Wooden Shoe's ed line exists for: history read as a toolkit, not nostalgia.
Wooden Shoe Books, 704 South St, Philadelphia, PA 19147
7:00 PM · Not listed
2
West Philly Canvass for Chris Rabb
Philly DSA canvasses West Philly for Chris Rabb, the democratic-socialist candidate running for PA-03 on universal healthcare, childcare, and abolishing ICE. No experience required — new canvassers get trained and paired with a partner, then the group debriefs together after. Direct electoral organizing, and a chance to talk to actual neighbors about actual policy.
Kingsessing Recreation Center
6:00 PM · Not listed
3
Dekalog: Parts 3 & 4
Kieślowski's Dekalog — ten hour-long films loosely built around the Ten Commandments, among the towering achievements of world cinema — gets a rare theatrical run at the Film Society Center, screened two parts a night across a special series (tonight: 3 & 4, in a 4K restoration). Not a streaming-queue watch: genuinely different on a big screen, and not a presentation you'll see return soon.
Philadelphia Film Society
7:30 PM · Not listed

Thursday August 20

⭐ Top 3 Picks

1
Killer Of Sheep
Charles Burnett's 1978 landmark of Black independent cinema — a loose, lyrical portrait of a slaughterhouse worker in 1970s Watts — gets a rare 4K theatrical screening as part of the Film Society Center's LA Rebellion series. One of the foundational texts of American independent film, and it essentially never plays theatrically; a genuine chance to see it as intended, not on a laptop.
Philadelphia Film Society
7:00 PM · Not listed
2
🔋 Beginner Soldering: Li-Ion Battery Pack
A hands-on, no-experience-required soldering class at Iffy Books: you leave with a working rechargeable battery pack for a microcontroller project (ESP32, Pi Pico, LoRa node), not just notes. $3.99 for the kit plus your own battery — about as low-stakes as DIY electronics gets, and exactly the kind of practical-skills programming that makes Iffy Books more than just a bookstore.
Iffy Books, 404 S. 20th St., Philadelphia, 19146, United States
2:30 PM · Not listed
3
Sip City Transcend
A free, no-cover queer social mixer built specifically for trans, non-binary, and gender non-conforming Philadelphians, running Thursdays at Tattooed Mom. 'We won't be erased. We've always been here' — community space as its own quiet political statement, not a themed party.
Tattooed Moms, Philadelphia, PA
6:00 PM · Free

AAAM26 After Dark: Jubilee: Celebrating the African American Museum in Philadelphia at 50
Free closing celebration for the Association of African American Museums' national conference, marking 50 years since AAMP became the first museum built and funded by a major U.S. city specifically to preserve Black history. Expect a performance from the Marian Anderson Choir and an interactive AAMP Disruption showcase — a genuinely rare night where the museum itself is the event, not just the backdrop.
